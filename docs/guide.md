# Get the OTB tunings onto your tuner, and use them

This page shows how to put the OTB tunings on a Peterson StroboClip HDC, one at a time, from Peterson's website. Each tuning takes about a minute, and you only need the ones you play.

There is no single install of all 20: Peterson's User Trading Post does not yet allow whole configurations for the StroboClip HDC, so the tunings go on one by one.

## What you need

- A Peterson StroboClip HDC, HDC-B or HDC-V. [Other models](compatibility.md).
- The tuner's USB-C cable. The HDC charges and connects by USB-C.
- A computer running Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge (or Chromium, Opera, Vivaldi, Comodo Dragon). Peterson Connect does not work in Safari.
- A free account at [petersontuners.com](https://www.petersontuners.com/).
- The [tuning card](../card/stroboclip-old-time-banjo-card_letter.pdf), open as a PDF or printed, for the three-letter codes.

## Before you start: what happens to the presets already on the tuner

Peterson's page says: "If you have not previously created a custom configuration, this will create a new custom configuration for your device and load the selected sweetener into the top of your sweetener list. This will be the only sweetener available unless you add more."

In plain words, your first tuning starts a list of your own, and that list takes the place of the factory presets on the tuner. The factory presets are never lost: on the tuner, open Settings, then Configuration, and choose `dEF` to get the factory list back. Your own list stays on the tuner too, in the same menu.

If you already have a custom list, each new tuning is added to the bottom of it. Nothing is replaced.

## Add a tuning from the User Trading Post

1. Plug the tuner into the computer and sign in at petersontuners.com. Click the round tuner icon at the top right of the site. If it says "No Device Detected", click **Find Device** and pick your tuner in the box the browser opens. When the panel says "StroboClip HDC Connected", you are ready.
2. Open the [User Trading Post](https://www.petersontuners.com/sweeteners/shared/) and expand **Misc. Instruments**.
3. Find the code you want. The tunings are listed by code, not by name, each marked "by Michael Marmor": `GST by Michael Marmor`, and so on. Click the **i** at the left of a row to see the full name and the strings, for example "Piney Woods: G (gGDGB)".
4. Click the **+** at the right of the row. Its tooltip reads "Add to my tuner now".
5. A box asks "Add PNW to Which of Your Devices?" (with your code in place of PNW). Click your tuner.
6. A second box reports "1 sweeteners(s) added." Click **Update**. The tuning is not on the tuner until you do.
7. Wait for the banner "Your StroboClip HDC is synchronized and up-to-date." before unplugging.
8. Repeat from step 3 for the next tuning.

Each new tuning goes to the bottom of your list. Starting from an empty list, add them in the card's order, row 1 first, and the tuner lists them in that order with no further work.

If the page says "Sweetener PNW is already loaded onto this device," that tuning is already on your tuner and nothing has changed.

## Colors, order and plain EQU

These are set on Peterson's **Configure Your Tuner** page, which you reach from anywhere on the site by clicking the round tuner icon at the top right and then **Configure Settings**, with the tuner plugged in. Everything on this page is optional.

**Colors.** A tuning arrives with no color of its own and shows in the tuner's general color. To give it one, hover over its row in the Sweetener Presets list, click the palette icon, and pick a color. The chips on page 1 of the card show the colors I use and why; pick the same ones or your own. Judge colors on the tuner, not on the screen: the screen swatch is not a reliable preview of the tuner's display.

**Order.** Drag a row up or down in the Sweetener Presets list with the mouse. On the same page, leave Auto Re-Order Presets off, or the tuner will move the presets you use most to the top.

**Plain EQU.** EQU is Peterson's plain chromatic preset: equal temperament, all twelve notes, no sweetening. I keep it first in my list, ahead of the 20 tunings. If your list does not have it, it is on the Configure page under **Default** in Your Sweetener Library, where Peterson describes it as "Standard chromatic tuning/Equal temperament ... no Sweetening"; hover over it and click the arrow at the right of its row to move it into your Sweetener Presets list. A red dot beside an entry means it is already in your list. On the tuner, pressing + and − together while the code flashes takes me straight to EQU.

**Removing a tuning.** Hover over its row and click the red **X**, "Remove from this custom configuration". It comes off your list only; it stays on the Trading Post and you can add it again.

After any change on this page, click **Update Connected Device** and wait for the synchronized banner.

## On the tuner

The tuner has three buttons: − , Menu (also power), and +.

| To | Do |
|---|---|
| Change tuning | Click Menu until the three-letter code flashes. Step with + or −. Click Menu again, or wait, and the code stops flashing. |
| Back to EQU, the plain chromatic preset | While the code flashes, press + and − together. This is how it works on my tuner, where EQU is first in the list. |
| Tune a string | Pluck one string at a time, with a finger rather than a pick. The strobe bands scroll left when the string is flat, right when sharp, and stand still when it is in tune. Come up to pitch from below. |
| See which tuning is active | The code shows at the lower right, in the color you gave that tuning or the tuner's general color. The tunings with a sweetened note also show the word SWEETENER above the code. |
| Keep the card's order | Settings, then Auto-Order (the large A): Off. With it On, the tuner moves the presets you use most to the top of the list. |
| Return to the factory list | Settings, then Configuration (the large C): `dEF`. The same menu takes you back to your own list. On my tuner my list is named `OTB`. |

Settings is reached by clicking Menu five times, until the gear icon shows, then +. Concert A (Menu four times) and Drop/Capo transposition (Menu three times) are saved per preset; leave them at 440 and 0 unless you know why you are changing them.

## What you will see, and what catches people out

**The tuner listens only for the five notes of the selected tuning.** A string that is far off reads as another note in the tuning, with a still strobe. Select Piney Woods with the banjo still in G Standard and the 4th string reads a steady D; its target is low G. Check the letter against the card before you trust the strobe.

**On big moves, get close by ear first,** then let the tuner finish. Piney Woods drops the 4th string seven semitones.

**Seven tunings are a whole step above another one;** the card's notes say which. Tune all five strings up two semitones, or stay in the lower tuning and capo at the 2nd fret, raising the 5th string two semitones by hand or with a spike at the 7th fret. Then select the higher tuning on the tuner.

**Weak or jumpy readings,** most often on the short 5th string: try clipping the tuner to the rim of the pot instead of the headstock. The headstock works; the rim is a little steadier. This is my experience with two banjos, not Peterson's instruction.

**Keep listening.** Tuning by ear is still tuning; the strobe is there to confirm it.

## If the tuner's list does not match

Peterson Connect compares the tuner with your saved configuration when you reconnect, and offers to update the tuner if they differ. If the tuner shows presets that should not be there, or is missing some, unplug it, plug it back in, open [Peterson Connect](https://www.petersontuners.com/connect/) and accept the update it offers.

## How my own list was built, for anyone who wants to adapt it

Everything was done in Peterson's browser tools, signed in at petersontuners.com.

1. **Design Custom Sweeteners** (Tools menu). Each tuning is one sweetener: a name of exactly three characters, a description, five notes entered with their octave (the designer sorts them low to high, so the short 5th string lands at the right end), a cent offset per note, and the box "Sweetener should ignore all other chromatic notes and only respond to the notes listed above" ticked. Note names were kept, not string numbers. G Minor's accidentals were set to flats so the tuner shows B♭. Peterson's "5 String Banjo" template gives G Standard as a starting point, and any sweetener of your own can be the template for the next one.
2. **Configure Your Tuner** (Peterson Connect). The preset list holds EQU first, then the 20 tunings in order. Each preset gets a display color here, not in the designer; the palette holds at most 15 colors, and a color must be in the palette before a preset can use it. The toggle under the list is set to "Do NOT include any additional factory presets", and Auto Re-Order is off. The configuration is named OTB with a short description. This is my own tuner's list; the Trading Post carries the 20 tunings, not the list.
3. **Update Connected Device** sends the list to the tuner. Judge colors on the tuner, not on the screen: Peterson's swatches are not a reliable preview of the LCD.

Capacity was not a problem: twenty-five five-note presets used a few percent of the tuner's memory once the factory presets were excluded.

## How these steps were checked

I walked the steps above on my own StroboClip HDC on November 10, 2026 (firmware 1.0.15, Chrome on Windows), by taking one tuning off the tuner and adding it back from the User Trading Post. Text in quotation marks is Peterson's wording on the screen that day. My tuner already had a list of its own, so I did not see the very first add on a tuner with only the factory presets; for that case the page gives Peterson's own note.
